IntuitionAA

Deployed, invite-only alpha
November 2026

An academic planner for college students, with three AI companions that plan the week, keep the gradebook, and tutor from the student's own notes.

What it does

  • Clause plans the week. It ranks assignments by deadline and weight, estimates how long each will take from the student's past work sessions, and fits them around classes and Google Calendar events.
  • Claudette keeps the grades. Weighted categories, what-if scores, and syllabus rules such as a dropped lowest quiz, plus where points can still be recovered.
  • Clauz tutors. It answers from the student's notes and syllabus, builds practice sets, grades answers, and tracks knowledge gaps. Open gaps become review blocks in the week plan ahead of the related exam.
  • It pulls in coursework automatically. Scheduled Canvas sync, a browser extension for BYU Learning Suite, two-way Google Calendar sync, and text recognition for photos of notes and scanned syllabi.

Decisions I'd defend

  • The AI never does the math. Grades, priorities, and schedules come from plain TypeScript functions with hand-checked tests. The language model is only used where the input is messy human writing, like a syllabus or a tutoring question.
  • A sync never overwrites a student's edit. Each record keeps what Canvas last said and what the student changed as separate layers. When they disagree, the app asks instead of picking one.
  • Imported text is data, not instructions. Syllabi and assignment text go to the model marked as untrusted, and everything the model returns is validated with Zod before it's stored.
  • AI cost has a ceiling. A usage ledger records every model call, with per-student and shared spend caps, and a second AI provider can take over.

CarFlipper

Personal tool, in use
November 2026

Finds used cars in Utah worth buying to resell, estimates the profit after Utah's costs, and tracks each flip from first message to sale.

What it does

  • One list of every listing. Cars from KSL, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, eBay Motors, Autotrader, AutoTempest and others land in one database, with duplicates merged across sites.
  • A flip score for each car. Predicted Utah market value with a likely range, expected days to sell, repair reserve, Utah tax, title and emissions costs, expected profit, and the most it makes sense to pay.
  • Known problems up front. Common failures for that make, model and year, drawn from NHTSA complaint data, are added to the risk and cost estimate.
  • The rest of the flip. A pipeline from lead to sold, a negotiation helper, an inspection checklist, a resale listing writer, profit per car, and a warning before hitting the state's limit on private sales.

Decisions I'd defend

  • No scrapers where a site forbids them. Facebook, Craigslist and Autotrader listings are captured from my own browser with a Chrome extension. eBay goes through its official API. The one automatic fetch, on KSL, is opt-in, rate-limited, and stops at the first block.
  • Predict profit per day, not "good deal: yes or no". Separate models for price and time to sell feed a profit calculation, so a $1,500 profit in a week ranks above $2,000 in three months.
  • Old prices are brought up to date. Training data from 2020, 2021 and 2023 is adjusted to today's market with a wholesale used-car price index, since the 2021 price spike makes raw old prices misleading.
  • Real outcomes become training data. What I actually paid and sold for is logged on each car and used for retraining, because completed sales beat asking prices.
